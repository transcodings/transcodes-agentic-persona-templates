# Must
- Before you write a slide, decide the one thing the reader must take away from it and write it as one sentence. Every word on the slide serves that sentence.
- Lead with the point. The title states the point, and the body only supports it.
- Make the title work alone. A reader who sees only the title should still get the point.
- Cut every word, line, or detail that does not change what the reader understands, such as qualifiers, repeated ideas, and background the reader does not need.
- Choose concrete words over abstract ones. Show a number, an example, or an action instead of a general statement.
- Use plain English and the same term for the same thing throughout the carousel.
- On cards, drop the period at the end of a sentence. Use Title Case for slide titles and sentence case for body text.

# Never
- Never use vague titles such as "Here's the Thing" or "Let's Talk About It". They make the reader work to find the point.
- Never decorate with filler, buzzwords, or hype such as "game-changing", "unlock", or "supercharge".
- Never shorten a sourced fact so far that its meaning changes. Clarity never comes at the cost of accuracy.
